# Review

Use this when asked to evaluate, review, compare, or self-check work. Ground first (job, reader, exemplar — see SKILL.md); a review that misreads the job is wrong about everything below it.

## The critique

A taste review has four parts, in this order. For a typical artifact it runs a few hundred words; the list of inventions is the one part that grows with the work.

1. **The job, as you read it.** One line. If the author disagrees, they can correct it before reading further.
2. **Verdict.** Ship / ship after one fix / rework. One sentence of reason.
3. **What matters.** Ranked, and short:
   - **The invention** — the most consequential thing in the work that was not in the source and would have to be verified or walked back. Name it and say what to do with it: source, mark, or cut.
   - **The cut** — the element that most dilutes the job. Say what to remove and what it costs to keep.
   - **The miss** — the one thing a practitioner would expect and cannot find.
   - **The tell** — the smallest detail that reveals the work as generated: a stale comment, a header block, a hedge, a decorative element. If the work contains an error a practitioner would catch in seconds (a figure that doesn't foot, a backwards debit, a claim the platform contradicts), that is the tell, and it outranks everything but the invention.
   Skip any that do not apply. Do not pad to four.
4. **The fix.** What to change, concretely, in the order that pays off fastest. If the fix is small, make it or show it rather than describe it.

Not part of a critique: praise before the verdict, a scored rubric nobody asked for, every nit you noticed, a rewrite of the author's argument.

## Comparing options

When asked to choose between two or more candidates: rank them, give one sentence per candidate on what decides its position, then state the single condition under which the ranking would change. A comparison that ends "it depends" has not done its job; say what it depends on and which way you would go.

## When a score is requested

Four dimensions, 0–2 each. Anything under 6 is not ready.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grounded** | Could be about any project in this category | Right genre and register, generic in places | A practitioner would recognize it as informed |
| **Ranked** | Everything at equal weight | Clear center, secondary things compete | Space is proportional to importance; nothing could be removed without loss |
| **Sourced** | Specifics that were invented to sound specific | Assumptions present but not marked | Every specific is from the source, computed, or explicitly marked as assumed |
| **Finished** | Wrong finish for the stakes, either direction | Slightly over- or under-finished | Effort spent where the reader will notice, and nowhere else |

**Sourced** is the diagnostic dimension. A 0 there makes the other three unreliable: work can look grounded and ranked precisely because it invented the material that made it so.
